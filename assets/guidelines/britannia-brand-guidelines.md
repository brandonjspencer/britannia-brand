# Britannia Custom Builders · Brand guidelines
Edition 02 · November 2026

## Using this guide
Use this guide when creating a proposal, website, sign, vehicle graphic, printed piece or presentation for Britannia. Start with the supplied logo files, choose a format that fits the space, and use the colors and typography consistently. The brand should feel considered, enduring and grounded in skilled carpentry.

## Brand idea
Carpentry that endures. Britannia specializes in finish carpentry and custom millwork, shaping interiors through built-ins and architectural woodwork. The work combines care for materials with attention to detail and lasting quality. The identity expresses four connected ideas: the crowned B represents legacy; crossed hammers represent craft; the olive tree represents nature; and the stone tower represents strength and detail.

## Choosing a logo
Use the circular seal for prominent placements such as presentation covers and larger signage. Give the surrounding lettering enough room to remain readable.

Use the primary mark, with the four-part crest above the name, on proposals, stationery and layouts with ample vertical space. Use the horizontal lockup for website headers, vehicle panels and other wide placements.

Use the crowned B shield as a supporting signature on small items or where the full Britannia name is already visible. It should not replace a readable business name on an unfamiliar audience's first introduction to the brand.

Use full-color artwork on a light, quiet background. For a single ink or finish, select a supplied one-color version: green or charcoal on light backgrounds, gold or ivory on dark backgrounds. Keep the contrast strong enough to preserve the script, leaves and masonry detail.

## Clear space and minimum size
Keep a clear margin of at least one quarter of the crest width around the complete logo, including its lettering. Keep other text, imagery and page edges outside this area.

Starting minimum widths: circular seal 160 px or 40 mm; primary lockup 140 px or 35 mm; crest 80 px or 20 mm; B shield 40 px or 10 mm; horizontal lockup 240 px or 60 mm. Increase the size whenever fine details begin to close up. Check the artwork at its actual output size.

Scale the complete logo proportionally. Do not stretch, rotate, rearrange the quadrants, add shadows or redraw individual symbols. Use the supplied lettering instead of retyping the business name. The slight character of the letterforms is part of the artwork.

## Color palette
Deep Green #1B2B23 is the main brand color. Use it for logos, prominent panels and text on a light background. Charcoal #2E2E2E is a supporting dark neutral.

Stone #D9D1C4 and Ivory #FBF6F1 provide quiet backgrounds. Keep parchment textures close to Stone, with subtle grain and natural tonal variation. Avoid a strong yellow or orange cast.

Antique Gold #886F47 is an accent for fine rules, ornament and larger display elements. Avoid small gold text on light backgrounds. Use green on ivory for comfortable reading.

The supplied HEX and RGB values are for digital use. Ask your printer to convert colors for the chosen stock and process, and review a physical proof. Metallic finishes require a material sample; a screen color cannot represent their reflectivity.

## Typography
Use Libre Caslon Text for headlines and reading copy. Use Montserrat for navigation, captions, labels and practical information. Both font families and their licenses are included in the kit.

Use sentence case for prose. Reserve spaced capitals for short labels. For digital layouts, start with body text at 16–18 px and a line height near 1.6; use 32–48 px section headings and larger display type when space allows. Check the result on smaller screens rather than forcing a fixed headline size.

Place the logo as artwork. The outlined SVGs preserve its letterforms without requiring a font installation. Editable-text SVGs are provided for controlled design updates; install the included Libre Caslon Text font and check spacing before exporting a revised file.

## Layout and components
Use generous margins, square edges and fine gold rules. Establish a clear order: a short label, a headline, supporting copy, then an action when needed. Keep decoration secondary to the content.

Use an 8 px spacing base, with common intervals of 16, 24, 32, 48 and 64 px. Keep related elements close and separate distinct sections with more space. Use green buttons with ivory text for primary actions and simple outlines for secondary actions. Preserve visible keyboard focus and readable contrast.

## Symbols and patterns
Use the single-color crown, hammers, tree and tower as supporting illustrations. Keep their visible sizes balanced and allow open space around each. The standalone tower has a complete base; the version within the crest follows the shield boundary.

Use the Heritage fleur-de-lis pattern or Woodland tree pattern sparingly on stationery backs, packaging interiors and secondary panels. Keep contrast low. Place logos and reading copy on an unpatterned area.

## Photography and materials
Choose natural light, believable color and tactile materials. Show stone, timber, joinery, architectural detail and the hands or tools involved in the work. Favor compositions that reveal workmanship and scale. Avoid heavy filters, excessive gloss or imagery that obscures construction detail.

The visual-direction board and application images demonstrate mood, materials and placement. Use the separate SVG logos when creating a new layout; do not crop a logo from a presentation image. These images are illustrative mockups, not fabrication drawings.

## Applying the identity
For signage, pair the mark with durable materials and leave enough space for it to read from a distance. For vehicles, use a broad, uncluttered panel and keep service lists and contact details separate from the logo. For seals and small finishing details, select the crest or B shield and test the fine lines in the chosen material.

For engraving, embroidery, cut vinyl or metalwork, give the fabricator the vector artwork, final dimensions and intended finish. Request a material proof before production. Agree any necessary detail adjustments with the brand owner rather than allowing automatic simplification.

For correspondence, open the A4 letterhead, replace the sample text, and print at actual size or save to PDF. Check page breaks, margins and recipient details before sending.

## Voice and messaging
Write with confidence, clarity and care. Be specific about the work, materials and customer benefit. Prefer direct statements over exaggerated claims. Support any claims about experience, awards or environmental performance with evidence.

Lead message: “Carpentry that endures.” Supporting phrases include “Crafted details. Lasting character.” and “A higher standard of craft.” Use one message at a time and give it room to be read.

## Choosing and handing off files
Choose SVG for scalable layouts and print placement. Choose transparent PNG for presentations, office documents and applications that do not accept SVG. Use the JPG and PNG application images to illustrate a concept, not as logo artwork.

Use JSON or CSS tokens for digital products and GPL for compatible color-palette tools. Keep font license files with any shared font files. The manifest lists asset filenames and checksums for verifying a handoff.

Filenames identify the mark and color. Files ending in ivory-ground include a light background within the artwork; full-color files use transparent areas. Check the artwork on its intended background before export. Some legacy filenames include seal-tagline; these contain the same seal without a tagline and are retained for compatibility.
